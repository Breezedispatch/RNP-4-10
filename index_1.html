Class I vs Class II

Class II is a dispatch decision, not just a chart note

Step 1 · Recognize

Does the route go Class II?

Any en route portion outside VOR / VOR-DME / NDB operational service volumes is Class II. Gulf crossings, West Atlantic tracks, and remote continental segments all qualify.

Step 2 · Verify

Can the aircraft do it?

Confirm the installed and operable LRNS satisfies the requirement. Check the AML for deferrals — a nav deferral can quietly change what class of route you can release.

Step 3 · Fallback

Class I is the escape route

Lost RAIM, deferred GPS, in-flight GNSS failure — the recovery is a Class I route: coastal, within NAVAID coverage. Know your Class I options for every oceanic city pair.

Common trap

Class II ≠ “far from land.” It's about signal coverage. A short overwater hop beyond service volumes is Class II; a long coastal route inside coverage is Class I.

RNP Standards

The number is the corridor

RNP Standards

Monitoring & alerting — not just accuracy

RNP 4 — true RNP

  • Self-monitors: EPU compared to RNP continuously
  • Alerts the crew (EICAS) when performance can't be met
  • GNSS (GPS) required
  • Supports the tightest oceanic routings

RNP 10 — a historical misnomer

  • No onboard monitoring or alerting
  • ICAO now labels it RNAV 10
  • Met by GNSS or dual INS/IRS
  • The minimum for our Gulf & West Atlantic RNP routes

RNP Standards

The four terms that decide legality

Navigation

LRNS

Long-Range Navigation System. GNSS (GPS) and IRS sensors, used through the FMS.

Two independent LRNS for oceanic RNP. A220 carries five (2 GPS + 3 IRS).

Communication

LRCS

Long-Range Communication System. Per Breeze OpSpecs, the A220 operates with a single LRCS — one HF radio.

Dispatch catch: an HF deferral means no long-range comm — read the MEL and keep the route within VHF coverage.

Performance

RNP 4

±4 NM, 95% of the time, with monitoring & alerting. Needs 2 FMS + 2 GNSS in nav mode. Files as PBN/L1.

Performance

RNP 10

±10 NM, 95% of the time, no monitoring/alerting. GNSS or IRS configurations qualify. Files as PBN/A1. Minimum for our RNP routes.

Why 384 NM

Breeze is not ETOPS certified on the A220. The aircraft must stay within 60 minutes of an adequate airport at single-engine cruise — calculated as 384 NM. No RNP capability or fuel load overrides it.

Filing & Release

The oceanic release: dispatcher checklist

1 · Weather & NOTAMs

En route, destination, and alternate(s) — full picture before release. Verify planned fuel.

2 · RAIM & equipment

Confirm a passing RAIM prediction. Check the AML — and read the MEL item for every nav/comm deferral. Two LRNS operable; HF (single LRCS) operative.

3 · Codes match capability

Item 10a (G, R, H, W) and Item 18 (PBN/L1 or A1) must reflect what the aircraft can actually do today — refile NONRNP10 if it can't.

4 · Route limits

Route clear of the 384 NM warning area (Fusion: User-defined Warning Areas → Admin-published) · Class II segments verified against operable LRNS · position-report fixes noted.
